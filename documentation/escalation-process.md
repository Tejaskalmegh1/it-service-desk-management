# Escalation Process

## Purpose

Escalation transfers an issue to a team or technician with the required authority, access, or specialist knowledge. Escalation is a normal part of L1 support; it is not a failure.

## When to escalate

Escalate when:

- The issue is outside L1 permissions or documented procedures.
- Initial troubleshooting has not resolved the issue.
- A specialist system, server, network, application, or security team is required.
- A high-impact outage or widespread issue is suspected.
- A change could create data loss, security risk, or service disruption.
- The ticket is approaching or has exceeded its applicable target.
- A vendor or third-party service provider must act.

## Suggested support tiers

| Tier | Typical responsibility |
|---|---|
| L1 | Intake, basic diagnosis, known fixes, documentation, routing |
| L2 | Advanced desktop, application, identity, or network troubleshooting |
| L3 | Engineering, infrastructure specialists, product/vendor-level investigation |
| Security team | Suspected compromise, malware, phishing, or data exposure |

Actual team names and responsibilities vary by organization.

## Handover checklist

Before escalation, include:

- Ticket ID and priority.
- Requester and best contact method.
- Affected device, application, service, and location.
- Start time, frequency, and scope.
- Exact error message, if available.
- Troubleshooting steps and results.
- Relevant screenshots or logs, only if approved and sanitized.
- Business impact and any workaround.
- Reason for escalation and the action requested.

## Example escalation note

**Ticket:** INC-1008  
**Issue:** Employee cannot connect to the office Wi-Fi.  
**Impact:** One employee currently affected.  
**Checks completed:** Confirmed Wi-Fi is enabled, verified the correct network name, restarted the wireless adapter, and tested another approved network.  
**Result:** The device still cannot obtain network connectivity.  
**Escalation reason:** Requires further wireless/network investigation.  
**Requested action:** Network support to review access-point connectivity and relevant authentication logs.

## Communication principles

- Tell the requester that the issue has been referred and explain the next step.
- Do not promise a resolution time unless an approved target is available.
- Keep ticket notes factual and professional.
- Follow up according to the organization's process.
